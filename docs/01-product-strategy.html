The idea in 30 seconds

The hard part of a plan like yours is the thinking at every meal: what can I have, what does it equal, does this count. The app takes that thinking away, so the only thing left for you is a tap that feels good.

Your plan becomes tokens

Each unit (1 carb, 1 protein…) is a colored token. A meal is a row of tokens. Eating fills them. You never count.

How it stays fast and effortless

Rule of thumb for the whole build: if a screen makes you think, it gets redesigned. Defaults do the work; every action can be undone, so nothing asks “are you sure?”.

What it does for you, automatically

  • Opens on the right meal for the time of day, and learns your real meal times.
  • Suggests your usual dish, or your nutritionist's example until it knows you.
  • Re-portions your favorites when a new plan changes the numbers. Three tortillas become two by themselves.
  • Counts any food from plain words, including tacos, tortas and restaurant plates.
  • Fits a meal to your plan in one tap (“Que cuadre”).
  • Plans tomorrow each evening; you just accept.
  • Keeps your kitchen and shopping list in sync: what runs out goes on the list.
  • Reads each new plan PDF or photo and shows you only what changed.
  • Works offline and syncs by itself.
  • Reminds you only when you forget, at your real meal times, two a day at most, and goes quiet if you ignore it.

Left out on purpose

Each of these adds taps, words or pressure. Several are what most diet apps are built around.

  • Calories and macrosYour plan speaks tokens. Numbers add thinking and are linked to obsessive tracking.
  • Streaks you can loseBroken streaks make people quit. You get rolling week dots instead.
  • Red colors, grades, “cheat meals”Shame makes people stop logging the meals that matter most.
  • Rewards for eating lessThe plan is the target. Under-eating is never celebrated.
  • A chatbot coachSlow and wordy. The AI works silently in the background.
  • Barcode scanner and food searchTyping “yogurt griego” is faster than browsing a database.
  • Photo-first loggingPhotos feel easy, but studies show people log less with them. Photos come later as an option.
  • Daily weigh-insWeight is optional and asked only at your nutritionist check-ins.
  • Water and exercise tracking (for now)Shown in your plan, not tracked. Easy to add once the core habit is solid.
  • Leaderboards, social feeds, app-icon badgesComparison and red badges create urgency, not habits.

Why this works

I reviewed meta-analyses, trials and design research before deciding anything. The short version:

  • Logging often matters more than logging carefully.People who opened their food diary more times a day lost more weight, while minutes spent didn't matter. Logging at least two eating moments a day is the best adherence marker.
  • Most lost weight comes back, and appetite is why.Appetite rises by roughly 100 kcal a day for every kilo lost, and hunger hormones stay shifted for at least a year. What wins is a routine light enough to keep for years.
  • Habits form from repetition in the same moment, and survive a missed day.About two months on average; one miss barely matters. Long gaps do, which is why comebacks are celebrated.
  • All-or-nothing thinking predicts regain.Flexible control does better than rigid rules, and self-compassion after a slip reduces overeating. Hence calm “+” tokens and a clean next meal.
  • Deciding before you're hungry leads to better choices.Ordering ahead cuts calories; structured meal plans with shopping lists improved weight loss. Hence tomorrow's menu.
  • Making the right thing easy beats telling you about it.Convenience nudges work about three times better than information. Hence pre-portioned suggestions and “Que cuadre”.
  • Surprise and feedback keep motivation yours; expected prizes erode it.So rewards are visual and unannounced, never points to “earn”.
  • Game mechanics help a little, and only when they serve the real behavior.Here every mechanic rewards logging, planning or coming back. Broken streaks reduce return rates, so there are none to break.
  • Fresh starts boost motivation.Your plan changes every 15 days, a built-in fresh start. Seasons use it.
  • Notifications help modestly and wear off.So: few, well-timed, and quiet when ignored.
  • Food pictures trigger cravings.The app uses token icons, not glossy food photos.
